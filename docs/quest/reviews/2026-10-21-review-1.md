# Review 1 (Oct 21): Chapter 1 and the main story of Chapter 2

*Independent review of `quest/` on branch `claude/new-session-43aoxw` at `b69ed58`. Reviewed from the repository:
the scripted route replayed line by line through the simulator, a second walk of both chapters following only the
gold arrow (a scratch walker written for this review, not kept in the repo), battle simulations of every scripted
fight and every mountain-path ambush with the party exactly as the route leaves it, the Game Over engine and its
tests, the maps and dialogue data, and the browser smoke test as far as it would run in a headless container.
Chapter 2's side quests and Chapter 3 were excluded, as the brief says.*

## Verdict

Chapter 1 and the main story of Chapter 2 are coherent and finishable with no soft-lock: a walker that follows
nothing but the gold arrow, talks to whatever the arrow points at and takes the first sensible choice reaches
`act2.complete` in 2,068 steps and 59 map changes, and the only places it needed to look around were inside the Echo
work. The show-don't-tell rule is kept everywhere I looked (the caney scene where Anaco is taken is the best-staged
moment in the game), the remembered choices all pay off, and the Game Over engine is better tested than the fights it
follows. Two things must change before anyone outside plays: Governor Thorne is the first fight in the game that the
Attack button cannot win (3 wins in 40 with plain attacks, 30 in 40 even at the maximum Grudge), after five fights that
all fall to plain attacks, so the chapter's climax is also the game's first wall; and the gold arrow goes quiet at
exactly the three moments the Echo asks the player to do something (it points at the tile they are standing on). The
code is sound enough for ten more chapters; what is not obviously sufficient is the content throughput, which is a
schedule question, not an engineering one.

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. Thorne is a difficulty cliff, and the Grudge cannot climb it (Major)

**Evidence.** Battle simulations, 40 seeds each, party as the route leaves it just before each fight (HP is not
carried between fights, `engine/battle.ts:958` `settleBattle` only consumes items, so every fight starts at full HP):

| Fight | Party | Attack only | Sunthread + Aimed Shot, no items | Same, with items |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| intro_boar | Gianna 4, Yari 4 | 40/40, 16 turns, 46% hurt | 40/40, 8 turns, 19% | 40/40 |
| pier_marines | 5, 5 | 40/40, 42% hurt | 40/40, 23% | 40/40 |
| caney_breach (L6) | 7, 7 | 39/40, 61% hurt | 40/40, 25% | 40/40 |
| village_patrol (L6) | 7, 7 | 40/40, 56% hurt | 40/40, 25% | 40/40 |
| **thorne_blockade (L9)** | **8, 8** | **3/40, 99% hurt, 1.9 downs** | 40/40, 34% | 40/40, 36% |

With the Grudge (`config.ts:216-217`, +10% per loss, capped at +30%), attack-only against Thorne wins 8/40 at +10%,
24/40 at +20% and 30/40 at +30%. Thorne is `level: 9` with `def 26` and `weak: ['light']` (`data/battles.ts:214-229`,
`291-306`); his escort is a level-9 marine with `atk 30, spd 20`. Gianna's plain attack uses `atk 13`.

**What the player experiences.** Every fight up to the sea cave can be won by pressing Attack, and most players
will have been doing exactly that since the boar tutorial (which does teach Sunthread, three hours earlier). At the
chapter's climax the same habit loses in 12 turns with both fighters down. The goddess sends them back; the fight
gets +10%, then +20%, then +30%, and a quarter of attack-only players are still losing at the cap. Nothing on screen
tells them why: Anissa's tips for this fight (`battles.ts:301-304`) are "The marines first, or Thorne first?",
"Surge!", "He's aiming! Guard!" and "Gigi, heal! Please!". None of them says the one thing that matters, that
Thorne hates the light.

**Fix (any two of these is enough).**
- Make Anissa's `start` tip for `thorne_blockade` the answer: "He hates the light, Gigi. Sunthread. Yari, the
  musketeer." The data already knows he is weak to light; the tip should too.
- Let the Grudge keep climbing for a fight lost three times (or raise `GRUDGE_MAX` to 0.5 for scripted bosses
  only). At +30% an attack-only party still loses one try in four; a cap exists so the fight stays a fight, but a
  player who has fallen three times has had the fight.
- Make an earlier fight ask for the spell. `village_patrol` is the obvious one: give the musketeer `resist` against
  plain blows, or give the sergeant 50% more HP so Attack alone takes twenty turns. Right now the four fights between
  the boar and Thorne teach nothing the boar did not.

### 2. The gold arrow goes quiet inside the Echo (Major for a first-time player, trivial to fix)

**Evidence.** The gold-only walk stalled (arrow pointing at the tile the walker stood on, nothing to confirm) at:
- `act1:first_echo`, goal `zetwal-house 14,6` (`data/quests.ts:54`): the arrow points at the shimmer; once the player
  swaps, it still points at the shimmer. The aide, at `12,4`, who starts `story.warroom_echo`, has no goal. Only
  Anissa's third hint names him (`quests.ts:58`), and that hint comes after six minutes stuck at the default timing (`config.ts:146-147`: 3 minutes, then every 90 seconds).
- `act1:read_orders` after an early return: if the player presses Echo again before talking to the aide (a natural
  thing to try on the first Echo), `warroom_return` fires (`dialogue/zetwal.ts:812-825`), sets `story.warroom_returned`,
  and the goal becomes `17,2` (`quests.ts:79-80`) while the orders are still in the Echo with the aide.
- `act1:bridge` after the pawl is pinned: the goal stays on the pawl at `31,9` (`quests.ts:94`) while the player is
  still in the Echo; the way out is the bridgehead shimmer at `27-29,9-11` (`tools/maps/zetwal-town.ts:166-172`), two
  tiles west, with no arrow on it.
- `act1:boar` in the Cane Road Echo (goal `3,14`, the cutter's stone is at `2,16`) and `act2:lock` in the batey Echo
  showed the same shape: arrow on the player, action one tile away or back through the shimmer.

**What the player experiences.** The arrow, which has been reliable for an hour, points at their own feet at the
exact moments the game is asking them to do something new. The Journal text does say "then talk to the people
there", but the arrow is what Oct 17's players said they followed.

**Fix.** Goals are flag-stepped already; add an in-Echo step to each. The `onEcho` scene of a zone can set an
`echo.*` flag (wiped on return, `engine/game.ts`), so: `first_echo` gets `{ if: { flag: 'echo.warroom' }, at: 12,4 }`
ahead of the shimmer goal; `bridge` gets `{ if: { all: [anchor.chain_bridge, echo.bridgehead] }, at: 28,10 }` ahead of
the pawl; `read_orders` should not accept `story.warroom_returned` as "orders in hand" (condition it on
`clue.night_orders`). The same pattern will come up in every chapter with an Echo puzzle, so it is worth doing once
in `guide.ts` as a rule ("when in an Echo and the objective's `echoGoal` holds, use it") rather than per objective.
Also make the gold-only walk a permanent test (`tests/guide.test.ts` only checks that the arrows agree with the
scripted route, which is why this was not caught): a walker that follows the arrow and fails on any stall outside an
Echo would have flagged every item above.

### 3. The occupied city is a fetch loop: Chapter 2's second half is 19 map changes for four objectives (Moderate)

**Evidence.** Route profile (steps are tiles walked; "trans" is map changes), measured on `routes/zetwal-act1.json`:

| Chapter 2 objective | steps | map changes | maps |
|---|---|---|---|
| escape | 89 | 3 | village > conuco > cave |
| **held** | **147** | **6** | conuco > village > customs > village > plaza > caney |
| visit | 65 | 4 | plaza > village > customs > cellar |
| word | 90 | 4 | customs > village > conuco > cave |
| blockade | 139 | 5 | conuco > village > beach > seacave > harbor |

Chapter 2 totals 1,023 steps and 37 map changes against Chapter 1's 798 and 18 after Batch D. The second half
(`held` to `blockade`) is 441 steps and 19 map changes, and the cave-to-village corridor through the conuco is walked
five times.

**What the player experiences.** After the strongest hour of the game (the attack, Anaco taken, the cave, the
areíto) the story stops moving and the player ferries between the same four maps collecting a seller, a ledger, a
checkpoint stamp, a guard, a visit, and a word back to Yari, with one or two lines at each stop (`held` has 23
lines across six map changes; `visit` and `word` have nine each). On a phone this is the stretch where people put
it down.

**Fix.** Fold `word` into `visit`: let Anaco's cell visit end with Caona or the head runner carrying the word to
Yari (the runner network is already established in `xaragua.ts:1232`, "word travels faster than marines"), so the
player goes from the cellar straight to the beach. And put one of the three `held` clues on the way rather than at
the far end: the ledger in the customs house is the natural one to keep, the seller and the guard could be the same
stop. That removes roughly 150 steps and 7 map changes without losing a line that matters.

### 4. Gianna surrenders Anaco to fewer men than she just beat (Minor, story logic)

**Evidence.** `dialogue/xaragua.ts:311-384`. The lieutenant's three marines flee ("Beaten by children!"), 900 ms
later the captain walks in with two men, and Anaco tells Yari "You are no use to me in chains" and is led away by
the same two while Gianna and Yari, who have just won, stand and watch.

**What the player experiences.** They have just been told, in the fight they won, that they can beat marines. The
scene then asks them to accept that three cannot be fought. Anaco's line carries it emotionally, and the staging is
excellent, but a player who thinks about it will ask why Yari did not shoot.

**Fix.** Make the captain's arrival overwhelming: four or six marines with muskets levelled at the elders, not two,
and a captain's line that names the threat ("Any arrow, and the elders die first"). The stage system handles it
(`async` enters are already used here). Then Anaco's "no use to me in chains" is a choice made under a gun, not
a plot requirement.

### 5. "Owned or Remembered" can be left dangling forever (Minor)

**Evidence.** `data/sideQuests.ts:129-150`: accepted by listening, `available` ends at `story.crown_attack`, and
nothing closes it. `engine/sideQuests.ts` has no failed or closed state. The elder stands only in PEACE
(`tools/maps/xaragua.ts:148`). A player who listens, walks off, and triggers the attack has an active quest with a
silver arrow pointing at an empty fireside for the rest of the game. Chapter 1 avoided this with the "everything
before sailing" gate; Chapter 2 has no gate.

**Fix.** Either gate the attack the way Chapter 1 gates sailing (Yari refuses to go to the council while the quest
is open; the elder is on the way) or give side quests a `closed` condition and a Journal line ("The fire is out. The
pilgrim is gone."). The second is the one that will serve ten more chapters.

### 6. The war-room return speaks of orders Gianna does not have (Minor, continuity)

**Evidence.** `dialogue/zetwal.ts:823`: on returning without the anchor, Gianna says "He keeps his papers under the
window seat. I should go back and leave the orders there." The scene fires on the first return regardless of
progress (`zetwal.ts:813`), so a player who pops out before the aide has spoken hears about orders they have not been
given.

**Fix.** Condition that line on `clue.night_orders` (or `story.warroom_echo`), with a third line for the early case:
"I was Grandfather. I need to go back; someone was bringing him something."

### 7. The arrow for the checkpoint sits one tile off the barricade (Trivial)

`data/quests.ts:422` points at `xaragua-village 23,3`; the barricade object is at `21-22,3`
(`tools/maps/xaragua.ts:159`). The gold walk stood at `23,4`, faced the arrow and found nothing until it looked
left. Move the goal to `22,4` or `23,3` to `22,3`.

### 9. The smoke test's hint check has no margin for a slow frame rate (Minor, test only)

`npm run build && npm run smoke`: 106 checks pass (keyboard, save codes, touch overlay, four phone viewports, full
screen, home-screen app, shop), then `hintsAndJournal` times out waiting 5 s for the first hint at `?hintms=1200`
(`tests/smoke.mjs:372`). Reproduced in isolation: the hint arrives after about 5 s, not 1.2 s, in headless Chromium.
`engine/hints.ts` counts play time from Phaser's frame delta in `field` mode only, so a throttled frame rate
under-counts time. On a real browser at 60 fps this will not happen, but the 5 s budget has no margin for CI. Give it
15 s, or drive the hint timer from wall-clock time while in `field` mode. A second run with that one section skipped
passed everything after it that it reached: the intro, the debate corner, the Game Over films (first line, Gianna's
"Like my mother?", the count and Rise, the Grudge line on the retry, in landscape and portrait), and the whole of
Chapter 1 in the browser, checked against the simulator after every key, through the first half of Chapter 2 (216
checks passed); the container's time limit stopped it at the areíto, not a failure.

## The owner's question: the secret items

**Keeping the Quill in Chapter 1 and the Conch in Chapter 2 is the right call**, and the D5 argument in the Oct 19
outline review stands: moving the Conch to Chapter 11 turns a mid-game map ability into a trinket. **But neither item
is built on this branch, so neither "earns its place" yet; the question is premature, and the questions file should
say so.** When they are built:
- The Quill's *Say Their Names* (revive every fallen ally, once per battle) is nearly useless in Chapter 1, where the
  party is Gianna alone until Yari joins, and Chapter 2's fights are all winnable without a revive. It earns its
  place from Chapter 4 or 5, when there are three or four fighters to revive. That is fine for a legendary that grows
  with its wearer, as long as the Chapter 1 puzzle is short (the design's "2 Moderate" is right) and its reward text
  does not promise more than a two-person party can use.
- The Conch's free sea travel and hidden coves are worth exactly as much as there are coves and sea routes after
  Chapter 2. On the plan, the first time a player can use it is the voyage out of Chapter 2 itself, which is scripted.
  Build it when there are two or more optional sea destinations in the chapters after it, not before; a map ability
  with nothing to do is worse than a deferred one.
- Both are after-release work in the plan. I would keep it that way: the Nov 22 build is twelve chapters of main
  story with two side quests each, and every secret-item puzzle needs engine work (the Conch's tides, the Quill's
  chime sequence) that competes with chapters.

## What works and should be kept

- **The staging.** Every event in front of the party is acted out: the engraver's walk to the Hall, the consul
  into the Chancellor's office, the cutter to the lamp, the dawn farewell with Anicette coming down the pier, the
  caney scene (marines flee, the captain enters, Anaco walks the length of the hall on his cane and is led out
  between two marines), the pier fight, Yari into hiding, the escape. The `act([...stage cues], speaker, line)`
  shape in the dialogue files is the right abstraction and it reads like a script.
- **Batch D's cut.** Chapter 1 went from 1,069 steps and 32 map changes to 798 and 18; the papers errand is now one
  visit with the engraver coming to you. The dawn farewell lands.
- **Remembered choices.** Every `choice.*` flag that is set is read later (eight flags; `farewell_why/fine` are
  read six times, `soup_bowl` four, `yari_everything` three, `envoy_waters` once in Chapter 2 at the council,
  `warned_watcher` and `yari_sure` once each). Small payoffs, but every one real.
- **Game Over.** The engine (`engine/gameOver.ts`) is pure, the count and Grudge are per save and per fight,
  "meant to lose" fights are excluded, and `tests/gameOver.test.ts` checks the things that usually break: that every
  fight has a way back in, that Rise never lands on a trigger, spawn or door tile, and that debates and rhythm locks
  never cause a Game Over. The goddess's six first-meeting lines and "Like my mother?" are the approved text.
- **Ambush balance.** All five mountain-path groups are 30/30 with plain attacks at both ends of their window
  (party level 5 to 7), and the window closes at the attack, so Gianna alone in the occupied city is never ambushed.
  Anissa is never a combatant.
- **The arrows, outside the Echo.** The gold walk crossed every door, found every giver and finished both chapters;
  mystery quests show with a nudge and a dot, and `guide.test.ts` proves every goal is on a real map and reachable
  through the doors that are open at the time.
- **The debates.** Both are well written, Reason is gated on reading, and the strong answer is not always the
  pious one (the third round of each is won by compassion). "Anissa: he believes in Elodie like we do, so why does he
  sound like the Governor?" is the best line in Chapter 2.
- **Phone layout.** The smoke test passes all four landscape viewports (844x390, 932x430, 667x375, 740x360): no
  scroll, every control at least 44 px, nothing over the picture, and portrait shows the rotate hint without
  overlap.
- **The engine split.** `src/engine` is pure and simulated end to end (`routes/`, `sim.ts`, `act.test.ts`);
  481 tests pass. The route file is the chapter's contract and the tests enforce the order of objectives,
  the fights, the cards and the end map. That is what will carry ten more chapters.

## Is the code sound enough for ten more chapters in 32 days?

The engine, yes. It is data-driven (quests, dialogue, battles, encounters, debates, side quests, maps all in
`src/data` and `tools/maps`), the simulator plays a chapter in under a second, and nothing in `engine/` is
chapter-specific. Two things to watch:

- `src/scenes/FieldScene.ts` is 2,316 lines and growing with every stage cue and UI feature; it will be the file
  where regressions hide. Pull the stage runner and the hint/guide drawing out of it before Chapter 4.
- Throughput, not soundness. Two chapters are 3,507 lines of dialogue and 932 lines of hand-written map code
  (`xaragua.ts` alone is 721), built Oct 13 to 21 with four outline-and-review rounds. Ten chapters at the same
  density are about 17,000 lines of dialogue and 4,000 of maps in 32 days, plus the per-chapter route, tests and
  reviews. The plan's "two side quests per chapter" is the right cut; the Echo-goal rule in finding 2 and a
  permanent gold-walk test are the two engineering investments that will save the most review time per chapter.

## What I could not check

- **The browser smoke test from the areíto to the end of Chapter 2** (the record lock, the proof, the escape and
  the blockade on screen). Everything before it passed in the browser (finding 9); the container ran out of time.
  The screenshots (Game Over, the boar with the Grudge line, the staged temple, bridge and study, phone and desktop)
  look right.
- **Feel.** Timing of the stage walks, music, the Echo filter and the sparkle, text speed on a phone. The sparkle
  code and the drawn fountain are present (`FieldScene.ts:345-375`, `assets.pixels.ts:1708`); I did not see them
  move.
- **Chapter 2's side quests and Chapter 3**, excluded by the brief. The dangling-quest finding (5) applies to
  whichever of them opens and closes with the attack.
- **Thorne with Grudge under a real player.** The simulation plays one of two fixed plans; a human who mixes Guard
  and items will do better than "attack only" and worse than "Sunthread every turn". The cliff is real either way.
- **The secret items**, which are not built.
