# THRONE OF BLOODLINES
### A Game of Legacy, Betrayal, and Conquest
*For 2-8 players. Playtime: ~60 minutes.*

---

> *In the age before the first age, long ere the eight houses drew blood from one another, there was Elodie alone: the goddess who wove this realm from nothingness and has watched it turn ever since. She does not rule. She remembers. Every house that rises, every crown that falls, every grudge borne from parent unto heir, passes beneath her unsleeping sight. The seat at the heart of this contest has never truly stood empty; she has dwelt there always, waiting to behold who among the living is worthy of her gaze. To win is no mere matter of claiming a chair. It is to compel a goddess to turn from all others gathered, and rest her eyes upon you alone.*

The throne at the center of the board is formally **Elodie's Throne**, called simply "the Throne" throughout these rules for brevity. Her presence also surfaces directly during play: a handful of the game's Instant/Event cards are hers alone (marked and called out in Section 7), moments where the goddess acts rather than fate.

## 1. OVERVIEW

Each player controls a Bloodline, a family line fighting for control of the throne. You don't control one hero for the whole game; you control a succession of them. When your character dies, their heir rises, inheriting everything the parent had plus something new. Death is not failure. It's how your family gets stronger.

There is no required cooperation. Alliances can form, but nothing enforces them, and nothing stops a knife in the back.

You win by building power across three pillars, Influence, Fear, and Wealth, then making your move on the Throne.

---

## 2. COMPONENTS

- 8 Bloodline decks (4 generation cards each: I, II, III, IV)
- 1 Shared Action Deck (126 cards: 71 Hand Cards, 6 of them Reactions, and 55 Instant/Event Cards)
- Heart Tokens (life total, also your attack power)
- Grudge Tokens (one per bloodline color/emblem)
- Resource trackers or tokens for Influence, Fear, and Wealth (per player)
- Game board: 18x18 grid with the Throne at center and 8 bloodline-owned tiles
- Player pawns (one per bloodline)
- A d6 (movement and combat-adjacent rolls)
- A round tracker (for the Sudden Death cap, Section 8)

---

## 3. SETUP

1. **Determine bloodlines by lottery:** shuffle the 8 Grudge Tokens (one per bloodline color/emblem) face down. Each player blindly draws one: this is your bloodline for the game. Take the matching Bloodline deck (all 4 generations, stacked, Gen I on top).
2. Each player's Gen I card shows their starting HP, starting ability, and their bloodline's passive.
3. Each bloodline has a fixed, pre-assigned **owned tile**, one of the 8 tiles on the board, matching your emblem. See Section 5 for the full map layout.
4. **Player count adjustment:** place all 8 bloodline tiles on the board regardless of how many players are in the game. **If a bloodline is not in play this session, nobody owns its tile; it becomes neutral and freely farmable by anyone**, no restriction. This means smaller games automatically have more open, unclaimed land, with no separate removal step needed.
5. Each player takes Heart Tokens equal to their Gen I max HP, and places their pawn on their **owned tile**.
6. Shuffle the Shared Action Deck. Each player draws 3 Hand Cards. **If any Instant/Event card is drawn during this initial deal, do not resolve or keep it; shuffle it back into a random point in the deck** and draw a replacement. Starting hands are always 3 Hand Cards, no exceptions.
7. **Turn order:** the oldest/wisest player at the table goes first, then play proceeds clockwise around the table for the rest of the game.

---

## 4. THE TURN

On your turn, in order:

1. **Move:** roll 1d6 to determine your movement range this turn. Move your pawn that many spaces in a straight line, orthogonal only (forward or to the side, no diagonals), and you must commit to one direction for the full roll (no turning corners mid-move).
2. **Resource Check:** If you end your move on a tile matching a resource type (Court = Influence, War = Fear, Trade = Wealth), gain 1 (or 2 if you're the only player on that tile). **Exception:** you cannot gain from your own owned tile, ever. **Exception:** you cannot gain from the same tile you scored from on your immediately preceding turn.
3. **Combat (optional, free action):** If an enemy pawn is adjacent to you, you may attack. **Opening truce:** no one may attack during the first round. This does NOT use up your card play for the turn: you can attack and still play a Hand Card in the same turn. Damage dealt = your current Heart Token count. Defender may play a Block/Deflect card in response (see Section 6).
4. **Play 1 Hand Card** from your hand, **or offer a trade** (optional, you may pass). A trade offer: 1 of your resources for 1 of a different kind from any other player, who may accept or refuse. Either way it is your action.

**Reactions:** Reaction cards are Hand Cards you hold for other players' turns. Each says the moment it can be played (when a rival rolls to move, plays a card, trades, takes from a tile, stops beside you, or targets you with a card). They are never your own action.
5. **Draw back up to 3 Hand Cards.** If you draw an Instant/Event card, resolve it immediately; it does not count toward your hand of 3, and does not get held. Keep drawing until you hold 3 Hand Cards.

**Hand size cap (always enforced):** your hand can never hold more than 3 Hand Cards, even briefly. If a Barter card, a forced-draw effect, or anything else would push you above 3, immediately discard your choice of cards back down to 3 before play continues. Discards from this go to the bottom of the discard pile as normal.

---

## 5. RESOURCES & TILES

Three pillars: **Influence**, **Fear**, **Wealth**.

**The map:** an 18x18 open board with the Throne at the dead center. The only resource tiles are the **8 bloodline-owned tiles**, one per bloodline, arranged around the Throne (3 Court for Influence, 3 War for Fear, 2 Trade for Wealth), spaced so no two same-type tiles are adjacent. There is no separate neutral-tile ring: at lower player counts, any bloodline not in play simply leaves its tile ownerless, and an ownerless tile is neutral and free for anyone to farm (see Section 3).

- Standing on a matching tile at end of turn generates that resource (see turn structure for exceptions: never from your own owned tile, never from the same tile you scored from last turn).
- **Movement:** roll a d6 each turn and move that many spaces in a straight line (orthogonal only, forward or to the side, no diagonals, no turning mid-move). Crossing the full board edge to edge takes roughly 3 turns of maximum rolls, giving real, felt travel time to reach a specific rival or tile.
- **Wealth scarcity is intentional but backstopped by cards.** With only 2 Trade tiles on the board, and neither owner able to farm their own, Wealth is the scarcest pillar and the one people will fight over hardest. To keep it a viable path to the throne even so, the shared deck includes more flat "gain Wealth" Hand Cards and Wealth-themed Instant events than it does for Influence or Fear (see Section 7).
- A small number of Hand Cards also grant flat Influence or Fear directly, no tile required, as an occasional safety valve, just proportionally fewer than the Wealth ones.
- **Tiles have no occupancy limit.** Any number of pawns may share a tile at the same time. Players sharing a tile count as adjacent to each other for combat (Section 6). A tile with 2+ players on it is "contested": everyone there still gets the base 1 resource, just not the solo 2-resource bonus.

*Note on terminology: "Trade" refers specifically to the Wealth-generating tile type. Cards that let you swap hands/cards with another player are a separate category called **Barter cards** (Section 7); the similar name is coincidental, so keep the two distinct at the table.*

---

## 6. COMBAT

- Combat is only possible between adjacent players. **Adjacent means the 4 orthogonal neighbors (up/down/left/right) on the grid, not diagonal.**
- **Damage = attacker's current Heart Token count.** A full-health attacker hits hard; a wounded attacker hits weak. This means the first solid hit in a fight often snowballs.
- Losing Heart Tokens reduces both your survivability AND your future attack power; there is no separate "attack stat."
- **Block/Deflect cards** are rare (8 in the deck) and are played off-turn, in response to being attacked, to negate or redirect damage.
- When a Heart Token total reaches 0, the character dies. Proceed to Section 9 (Death & Succession).
- **Simultaneous deaths:** if a counter-effect (like a Riposte card) means both combatants drop to 0 Heart Tokens from the same exchange, both characters die. Resolve Death & Succession (Section 9) for the original attacker first, then the defender; this only affects the order you physically do the bookkeeping in, not the outcome. Each still places a Grudge Token against the other as normal.

---

## 7. THE SHARED ACTION DECK

Two card types, one shared deck:

**A. Hand Cards** (drawn, held up to 3, play 1 per turn)
- Resource cards (gain Influence/Fear/Wealth)
- Rare ranged/cursed attack cards (e.g., "Cursed Dagger: attack any player on the board regardless of distance, deal 1 damage")
- Disruption cards (force discards, steal resources, swap hands)
- Movement/positioning cards (bonus movement, teleport to a visited tile)
- **Barter cards** (forced or optional card/resource swaps between players, e.g., *"A traveling merchant offers a trade: switch one card in your hand with the player to your left."* Not related to Trade tiles/Wealth specifically.)
- Temporary truce cards (non-binding alliances, short duration)
- Block/Deflect cards (rare, off-turn use)
- Reaction cards (6: played on another player's turn at a set moment, e.g. cancel their move or their card)

**B. Instant/Event Cards** (drawn, resolve immediately, never held)
- Dice-based chaos affecting one or all players (gain/lose resources, gain/lose HP)
- Global effects ("everyone loses 1 Heart Token")
- Targeted effects based on game state (e.g., "the player closest to the Throne loses 1 Influence")
- Hand disruption (forced reveals, forced swaps)

**Deck composition (126 cards total): 71 Hand Cards (including 8 Block/Deflect, 6 Reactions and 5 ranged-attack cards), 55 Instant/Event cards.** The full card-by-card list lives in `bloodline-card-list.md`.

**The deck size and composition stays fixed regardless of player count.** No scaling up or down the Instant/Event ratio at lower player counts; the only adjustments for player count are the number of tiles in play and the win threshold (Section 8).

**Running out of cards:** when the draw deck is empty and a player needs to draw, shuffle the entire discard pile (all previously played Hand Cards and resolved Instant/Event cards) to form a new draw deck, and continue drawing without pausing the turn. This can happen multiple times over the course of a game.

**Elodie's hand:** 10 of the 55 Instant/Event cards are the goddess acting directly rather than impersonal fate; they stay mechanically identical to their category (mostly Global Effects and Targeted cards) but are called out with distinct flavor text and, for the final physical print, a distinct card back/border so they visibly stand apart when drawn. See the card list for which ten and their renamed titles.

## 8. WINNING THE GAME

1. Track Influence, Fear, and Wealth individually per player (no conversion between them by default, aside from specific cards).
2. Once a player reaches a combined threshold (**suggested: 10 total across all three pillars, or 6 in a single pillar**), they become eligible to claim the Throne.
3. On a future turn, the eligible player must physically move their pawn onto the Throne tile and declare their **Claim**.
4. **Challenge:** Any OTHER player who has independently also reached the win threshold may immediately declare a **Challenge** in response to the Claim: no travel required, the challenge represents rushing to intercept the claimant. A Challenge must be met: the claimant and challenger fight it out immediately (damage = current Heart Tokens, as normal combat). If there are multiple challengers, resolve them one at a time, in turn order starting from the claimant's left.
   - If the claimant defeats all challengers, they win the game immediately.
   - If a challenger defeats the claimant, the claimant dies as normal (Section 9) and loses their claim. The victorious challenger does NOT automatically win; they must still travel to the Throne themselves on a future turn to declare their own Claim (unless they happen to already be standing on it).
5. **If no one challenges**, the claim succeeds immediately and the claimant wins.
6. **Lower player-count adjustment:** at 2-3 players, reduce the threshold slightly (suggested: 8 total / 5 in a single pillar) to keep pace with less natural disruption.
7. **Simultaneous eligibility:** since Claims only happen on your own turn, true simultaneity is rare, but if an Instant/Event card pushes two or more players over the threshold in the same round before anyone has claimed, whoever's turn comes up next (in normal turn order) gets first chance to travel to the Throne and declare. Reaching the threshold doesn't force a Claim; it just unlocks the option whenever that player is ready and in position.

### Sudden Death (end-of-game safety valve)

Track rounds (one round = every player has taken one turn). **Suggested cap: 16 rounds** (raised from 15 after Reaction cards slowed games) for a roughly hour-long game; adjust up or down after your first playtest depending on how fast turns actually move at your table.

If no one has successfully claimed the Throne by the end of the capped round, the game ends immediately:

1. **Highest combined resource total (Influence + Fear + Wealth) wins**, no Throne trip required.
2. **Ties break in this order:** (a) highest single-pillar total, (b) most kills made this game, (c) most Grudge Tokens successfully settled (Reckonings), (d) if still tied, the tied players immediately duel (damage = current Heart Tokens as normal); last one standing wins.

---

## 9. DEATH & SUCCESSION

When a character's Heart Tokens reach 0:

1. The dying player loses half (round down) of one resource pool; their choice which pool bleeds.
2. Place a Grudge Token (in the killer's bloodline color) in front of the dead player: this marks unfinished business, unless this kill was itself a Reckoning (settling an existing grudge, see below), in which case no new Grudge Token is created. Settling a grudge ends the vendetta; it doesn't restart it in the other direction.
3. The player flips to their next Bloodline generation card (II, III, or IV), which lists the new ability gained (in addition to everything from prior generations) and any passive upgrade.
4. Take Heart Tokens equal to the new generation's max HP.
5. **Teleport your pawn back to your owned tile.** Your new generation always starts fresh at the family seat, regardless of where you died.

**The Grudge Mechanic (Reckoning):** If a player's heir kills the player who is holding a Grudge Token against them (i.e., you're killing the person who killed your parent), this is a **Reckoning**. Effects:
- The Reckoning bonus: immediately steal 1 point from any ONE of the defeated player's three resource pools (your choice which).
- The Grudge Token is discarded, and (per above) no new Grudge Token is created from this kill; the vendetta is settled and over.

## 10. WHEN GENERATION IV DIES: THE SPECTER

When a player's Gen IV character dies, they do not fully leave the game. They become a **Specter**:

- No HP, no pawn on the board, no resource pools of their own, no path to winning.
- Once per round, may play one Instant/Event card taken from the top few cards of the discard pile, targeting any player on the board.
- **Once a Specter plays a card, it is removed from the game permanently.** It does NOT go back into the discard pile or the deck. Specter cards are a one-time-use, finite resource (the pile of available cards to choose from only shrinks over the course of the game).
- **Specter powers are strictly mischief: no damage, ever.** Effects are limited to things like forced movement, hand disruption, and resource drains. Example: a Specter card might read *"Choose a player: teleport their pawn back to their owned tile."* This keeps a bitter eliminated player able to meaningfully annoy/redirect the table without being able to snipe the eventual winner out of spite.
- Grudge Tokens held by a Specter become void/flavor-only; a Specter cannot attack, so there's no way to formally settle a Reckoning. (Optional flavor: a specific rare Specter card could let them "curse" the person who holds a grudge against them with an extra-strong disruption effect, but this remains mischief-only, never lethal.)
- **If the discard pile runs low:** a Specter chooses from the top 3 cards of the discard pile. If fewer than 3 cards are available there (including right after a deck reshuffle empties the discard pile per Section 7), that Specter simply skips their mischief action for the round; they don't reach into the live draw deck. The option comes back naturally once more cards get discarded through normal play.

## 11. THE 8 HOUSES (Summary)

Each house carries a distinct cultural identity, homeland, and backstory woven into its abilities. Full lore and complete Generation I-IV ability text for all 8 live in the standalone reference file `bloodline-houses.md`; the table below is the quick mechanical summary.

| House | Homeland / Culture | Identity | Curve | Passive (Gen I to upgrade) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Brasador | Ardencia, Spanish-inspired | Fear/aggression | Early-favored | Sangre Ardiente: +1 Fear on kill, then +2 at Gen III |
| Dorini | Al-Doria, Mediterranean/Middle-Eastern | Wealth/economy | Late-favored | Baraka Dorini: +1 Wealth from trade actions, then +2 |
| Ironvow | Skarragol, fused Nordic/Mongolian | Mobility/aggression | Early-favored | Ironvow's Oath: -1 movement cost, then also -1 attack cost |
| Suzumori | Kuroshi, Japanese-inspired | Info/control | Mid-favored | Kagemimi: view 1 hand/day phase, then view hand + resources |
| Yaguana | Xaraguá, Taíno-inspired | Adaptive/chaotic | Swingy | Voice of the Opía: inherit a trait from any dead bloodline, then draw 2, pick 1 |
| Kay Soley | Zetwal, Kreyol/Haitian | Influence/diplomacy | Late-favored | Limyè Elodie: +1 Influence from court actions, then +2 |
| Agnivansh | Jwaladesh, Indian-subcontinent | Aggro/tempo | Early-favored | Agni ki Shakti: -1 ability cost, then also +1 Fear on any combat win |
| Stillwater | Aldermoor, English-inspired | Defensive/patient | Late-favored | Stillwater's Patience: block 1 Reckoning/game, then recharges each game |

**Balancing soft downsides (Early-favored houses):**
- Brasador: becomes the visible "most Feared" house by Gen III-IV, granting others a small bonus specifically when attacking them.
- Ironvow: their Gen IV double-turn ability forces a full hand reveal.
- Agnivansh: lowest HP ceiling of all 8 houses by Gen IV (glass cannon).

## 12. REMAINING OPEN QUESTIONS

All structural/edge-case gaps are resolved (hand-size overflow, deck reshuffling, tile stacking, Specter card exhaustion, simultaneous deaths, and the Sudden Death round cap). The full 120-card list is drafted separately (see `bloodline-card-list.md`), and the full house lore, homelands, and Gen I-IV ability text are drafted in `bloodline-houses.md`. What's left is tuning, not rules:

1. **Multiple challengers at the Throne**: if 3+ players are all threshold-eligible when a Claim is made, resolving fights one at a time (per Section 8) could take a while. Worth a quick playtest to see if this drags, or if it's actually the exciting climax it's designed to be.
2. **Sudden Death round cap**: 16 rounds is the current estimate; confirm with an actual playtest and adjust.
